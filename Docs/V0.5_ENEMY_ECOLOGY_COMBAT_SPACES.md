# Ashbound v0.5 — Enemy Ecology & Extended Combat Spaces

This milestone adds a data-driven enemy ecology and graybox combat spaces while preserving the v0.4 Hub, equipment rewards, elemental builds, local multiplayer, boss, corruption, and final encounter. Values and dimensions are prototype inputs; enemy balance and map scale still require human playtesting.

## 1. Enemy architecture changes

`EnemyDefinition` owns identity, role, combat stats, preferred range, affinity, resistances, targeting, movement, attack, spawn presentation, reward tier, elite flag, telegraph language, and prefab/VFX/audio hooks. `EnemyBrain` coordinates target selection and cooldowns. Ten `EnemyRoleBehaviour` strategies own role decisions, while `EnemyElementRuntime` adds affinity mechanics after an attack. `EntityFactory` now creates enemies from definitions; `EnemyKind` remains only as a compatibility lookup for older room data and tests.

`Combatant.Targetable` supports burrow states. Enemy status and stagger resistance feed the existing `StatusEffectController`, `ActorMotor`, and `CombatService` paths rather than creating a second combat model.

## 2. Base enemy roles implemented

The ten base definitions are Cinder Warrior, Ash Bruiser, Veil Assassin, Lantern Ranger, Bell Mage, Vault Flyer, Grave Burrower, Ember Bomber, Ash Choir, and Chain Keeper. The Cracked Warden is an additional elite compatibility definition for the existing mini-boss node.

## 3. Elemental enemy variants implemented

Ten variants prove role-plus-element composition: two each for Fire, Frost, Lightning, Poison, and Void. Fire adds Burn and temporary denial, Frost adds short Chill pressure, Lightning chains to a nearby player, Poison applies DoT and a brief hazard, and Void applies a mark and limited displacement. Normal enemies remain primarily role-defined.

## 4. Encounter templates created

`EncounterDefinition` supports groups, counts, presentation, start delay, interval, reinforcement flag, difficulty, risk, element pressure, reward tier, arena requirement, preferred diameter, and target duration. Authored templates are Frontline Pressure, Collapse, Vertical Threat, Ground Ambush, Control Group, and the mini-boss Cracked Trial.

`RegionEnemyPoolDefinition` provides future common, hard, elite, element, and encounter pools. Ash Ruins, Frozen Reach, and Void Depths are structure examples only; they are not complete regions.

## 5. Enemy behavior differences

- Warrior alternates readable basic pressure with a periodic heavier strike.
- Bruiser uses slow, wide knockback pressure.
- Assassin circles toward flanks, then commits along a visible dash line.
- Ranger kites and maintains sustained aimed projectiles.
- Mage targets predicted ground space with delayed AOE.
- Flyer orbits at visual height, descends for a telegraphed dive, and exposes a melee window.
- Burrower becomes temporarily untargetable, marks an emergence point, then erupts there.
- Bomber uses a long countdown and also produces a death burst that can trigger nearby Bombers.
- Support chooses a wounded ally and applies bounded healing/shielding on a long cooldown; alone it falls back to a weak projectile.
- Controller applies a short slow and pull inside a delayed field.

## 6. Combat-space categories

Small, Medium, and Large are explicit data categories. Each space also records technical bounds, camera size, multiplayer separation guidance, entrance, exit, and transition length.

## 7. Irregular arena layouts created

Five layouts are authored: Crooked Threshold, Twin Courts, Branching Ruins, Broken Ring, and Divided Hall. Each is assembled from several offset playable sections with a polygonal visible boundary and sparse solid obstacles.

**Ashbound combat arenas must not default to visually obvious square boxes.**

Rectangular technical bounds are acceptable, but visible geometry must break them with irregular edges, terrain, architecture, obstacles, connected subspaces, natural boundaries, and background extension.

## 8. Connected-space implementation

`CombatSpaceSection` lets one encounter contain several floor regions. Twin Courts joins two courtyards with a bridge, Branching Ruins adds three usable branches, and Divided Hall uses parallel halls with cross-connectors. This is intentionally lightweight and does not introduce a navigation-mesh dependency yet.

## 9. Transition-space implementation

Every authored space includes a marked transition section and an exit beyond the main combat pocket. The existing sealed-exit interaction remains the node boundary, but the player now crosses a short path or corridor before changing nodes. Timing fields also allow reinforcement entry from connected spaces.

## 10. World-extension hooks

Combat spaces expose environment, boundary, and distant-background prefab slots plus named distant landmark hooks. Current graybox silhouettes show where towers, statues, chasms, vaults, ruins, and skylines can later replace primitives without changing combat logic.

## 11. Multiplayer spatial concerns

Medium and Large definitions increase camera framing and record a suggested separation limit for future party constraints. Their subspaces support spread, flanks, and ranged spacing, but they still use one shared fixed-center camera. Physical 2–4 player sessions must verify visibility, regroup paths, AOE coverage, and whether players can leave useful framing.

## 12. Telemetry changes

Local schema version 4 records kills by enemy role, damage and player deaths caused by role and element, encounter composition, duration, player damage taken, average remaining living-player HP, arena category, elite outcome, and sampled party separation. Nothing is uploaded.

## 13. Performance risks

Enemy target selection is throttled and scans the registered `CombatService.Actors` list without scene-wide lookups or per-scan arrays. Support selection also uses that registry. Current spawning, projectiles, telegraphs, and primitive graybox pieces still instantiate/destroy objects; their APIs and prefab fields are the future pooling seams. Profile representative four-player encounters before adding pooling.

## 14. Files added

New runtime types live under `Assets/Scripts/Enemies`, `Assets/Scripts/Rooms`, and `Assets/Scripts/Core/Domain`. Generated definitions live under `Assets/ScriptableObjects/Enemies`, `Encounters`, `CombatSpaces`, and `RegionEcologies`. This guide and associated Unity `.meta` files are also new.

## 15. Files modified

The main integrations are `EntityFactory`, `Combatant`, `CombatService`, `StatusEffectController`, `RoomDefinition`, `RoomDirector`, `RoomView`, `ArenaCamera`, `PrototypeCatalog`, `PrototypeContentBuilder`, `RunManager`, `DebugMenu`, `MatchTelemetry`, `ActorView`, and the EditMode/PlayMode suites. `EnemyController` was removed after its responsibilities moved into strategies.

## 16. Human playtest checklist

1. Play each role alone and identify its attack from the telegraph before it lands.
2. Verify melee can punish a Flyer during its landing window and that the hover read is clear.
3. Verify Burrower position, emergence location, and vulnerable timing are understandable.
4. Test two and three Bombers for readable countdowns and fair chain reactions.
5. Confirm Support changes target priority without stalling an encounter.
6. Measure Controller, Frost, Void, and stacked-control frustration; confirm control stays short.
7. Play all five encounter templates solo and with 2–4 humans; record damage spikes and priority confusion.
8. Check Small, Medium, and Large spaces at common aspect ratios and with real gamepads.
9. Check obstacles, projectile collision, spawn entrances, exit paths, and camera visibility.
10. Complete the boss/corruption/final flow and inspect one schema-4 telemetry JSON.

Do not treat automated tests as evidence that enemy balance or map scale is correct.

## 17. Recommended next milestone

Run repeated solo and local-coop playtests, tune role overlap and space scale from schema-4 telemetry, then add navigation/avoidance and dynamic multiplayer camera framing where measurements justify them. After those foundations settle, replace one region's graybox environment and enemy silhouettes through the existing hooks before expanding to the full campaign.
